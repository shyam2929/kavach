1. The arithmetic of "Rs 10k a day on Rs 1L"

Rs 10,000/day on Rs 1,00,000 is 10% per day. Without compounding that is ~2,500% a year; compounded over 250 trading days it is 1.1250 ≈ 2×1010. For scale, SEBI found ~91% of individual F&O traders lost money in FY25, and 97% of traders in its behavioural sample were mainly option buyers. A target like that does not describe an edge. It sets the leverage, and the leverage sets the risk of ruin.

2. Where an edge actually exists: the volatility premium

An option position's P&L is, to first order, ½·Γ·S²·(realized variance − implied variance) over its life. On 2,174 days, India VIX exceeded next-week realized Nifty volatility 81% of the time, by a median of 3.5 vol points. The premium compensates sellers for rare, violent losses (worst week -75 pts) and has narrowed in 2026.

Forecasting realized vol (walk-forward HAR model) did not predict a larger premium. But a simple stress gate, fixed in advance at the 90th-percentile cut-points, separated the bad weeks:

5. Limits of this report

Premiums are model prices from VIX with an assumed smile, not historical option quotes. Weekly ATM IV can sit above or below VIX; the IV multiplier is the largest single source of error. The paper runner measures it live from Upstox chains (dashboard: IV calibration); once enough samples exist, set that multiplier and rerun research before trusting any rupee figure. Holidays are inferred from the data. Overnight gaps are captured only at daily resolution.
